# Mailcast evolution timeline, 5 October to 7 November 2026

Sourced timeline of how pdn-mailcast (packet-net/pdn-mailcast) changed from its first on-air slot to 7 November 2026, and why. Written for the history section of the paper. Plain ASCII.

## Conventions

- Times are UTC. Release dates are the tag dates from `git for-each-ref` in this repository (v0.3.0 and the v0.11.0 rcs were tagged in +01:00; converted here).
- "On air" means the first slot in the head end journal (data/headend-gb7rdg/headend-journal-to-2026-11-07.txt, cited as "J" plus the line's timestamp) that shows the change. A release is not the same as an install: GB7RDG's head end skipped many versions (it never ran 0.4.x, 0.9.0, 0.10.x or 0.11.x, for example).
- K is the number of source pieces a bulletin needs; "1.2 K" means 1.2 times that many pieces sent.
- Sources: "rel vX" = GitHub release notes; "PR #N" / "issue #N" = packet-net/pdn-mailcast unless another repo is named; "DL" = the maintainer's private decision log (working notes of Tom's decisions, not published; lower weight than issue or release text); "D" = the #packet-hf Discord channel export of 7 November (kept privately; time converted to UTC, message id where useful) or the notes file paper/notes/discord-packet-hf.md, which is already in UTC.
- "[paraphrase]" marks a reason put in my words; anything in quotes is verbatim.
- "Who asked": Tom (M0LTE, the operator), a named listener, or "measurement" when the change came from on-air or simulated figures. Changes built by Claude (an AI coding assistant) that Tom approved are under Tom.

## 1. Phases

**Phase 1, 5 October: from a one-day test to hourly daylight slots (v0.1.0 to v0.4.0).** The first slot (06:59) and a 10:15 retry were inaudible or nearly so, and the full test at 11:52 was wiped out because the 7.050-7.053 MHz channel sits on top of the UK 40 m packet channels. The signal was moved up to a 7.0538 MHz centre, cut into 240-byte pieces in 18 s bursts, and the modem's drive was doubled; the 15:17 and 16:00 tests then rebuilt all six bulletins at most web SDRs. The same evening Tom moved from one slot a day to hourly slots, then restricted them to daylight because the 07:12 and 18:00 slots were heard by nobody.

**Phase 2, 6 to 9 October: a sending strategy that fills the slot, and instruments to see the path (v0.5.0 to v0.8.7).** The first daylight day showed the share-based plan wasting airtime (one slot keyed nothing) while weak listeners got little, so the head end began filling every slot to a fixed airtime budget, least-covered bulletin first, and alternating 1200 bps (WN4) and 600 bps (WN3) slots. At the same time Tom added observe-only readings (ionosonde, PSK Reporter), a channel probe after the tone, a daily report from each receiver (first a personal mail, then a public MCAST bulletin), a dial setting for narrow-filter rigs, and more directory frames after G7TAJ's list stayed four days old.

**Phase 3, 9 to 20 October: transmitter identity, listener setups, trust, and the end of carrying (v0.9.0 to v0.16.2).** The transmit callsign moved from GB7RDG to M0LTE on 11 October because GB7RDG's licence (its "FAC") did not strictly cover this use. Receivers gained new audio sources (a local pdn-soundmodem, SDRconnect) and better radio sharing with LinBPQ. After the 14 October finding that most retired bulletins had gone at 6 K while weak receivers sat on frozen partial bars, spare airtime carried bulletins on to 10 K or 48 h, receivers learned to give up on bulletins no longer sent, and the directory was signed. Daily report receipts followed Jim GM4CXF's question. The web SDR survey (8 UberSDRs, 6 to 20 October) ran through this phase.

**Phase 4, 22 to 27 October: any station can transmit (v0.16.3 to v0.21.0).** With John G8BPQ preparing a second station (40 m at the half hour and QO-100), the head end lost its GB7RDG defaults, slots could start at any minute, each station signed its own directory and could advertise others, and receivers followed every station they knew. The modem's probe pattern was brought into line with MIL-STD-188-110D, and the head end began keeping each slot's run-up audio to check its frequent "channel busy" calls.

**Phase 5, 28 October to 1 November: Model B, combinable pieces from many sites (v0.22.0 to v0.30.0).** A survey of real bulletin copies across BBSes showed most copies of a bulletin are identical after a few rules, so bulletins are now sent in a canonical form with a separate "envelope", cut at a fixed 240-octet piece size, in each station's own scrambled piece order, so pieces from different sites add up. Every head end now signs and receivers refuse unsigned directories; a head end can relay what receivers on its LAN heard; and on 1 November nothing about any station (including M0LTE) remained in the receiver's code. Listener-driven usability changes (LAN page by default, PKTUSB for shared rigs) came in the same week.

**Phase 6, 2 to 7 November: retuning the strategy from the daily reports (v0.31.0 to v0.33.1).** A survey of 42 daily reports showed that only the strongest listener kept up and that one bulk sender (LU9DCE's digests) was 65% of bulletins taken in. WN4 slots moved to the long interleaver, the burst limit went from 18 s to 31 s, and bulk senders were put strictly last; a proposed "concentrate on a few bulletins" rule was simulated and dropped. The head end can now ask pdn-soundmodem to tune the rig, and receivers gained XRouter delivery.

## 5. Open questions (sources disagree or reason not recorded)

1. **Why the long interleaver, and why then.** Issue #234 (opened 2 Nov 08:19) records Tom's decision "(WN4 long-interleaver trial: not now.)", yet PR #236 was opened at 08:41, merged 09:20 and on air at 11:00 the same day. PR #236 calls it a "trial ... with real mail" and docs say it "rides out longer fades"; no measurement is cited. The MS110D path tests of 30 Oct (SA6DAZ in Sweden, all waveforms, short interleaver, per the path tests' own notes) may be the background, but I found no record linking them. Ask Tom.
2. **Why the burst limit went from 18 s to 31 s** at 10:15 on 2 Nov. Not in any issue, PR or the decision log; only the journal line and a note in the decision log ("maxBurstSeconds 31 in both configs"). The 4-block arithmetic is my inference. The measured effect (WN3 frames per slot 73-75 -> 85-87; long WN4 161 frames vs about 120 estimated at 18 s) is in the journal.
3. **Why hourly slots** (5 Oct evening). DL says only "Tom wants HOURLY slots"; PR #17 describes but does not justify. Discord 2026-10-06 09:41 ("maximise the number of chunks a station will hear on average over a 2-3 day period") is about the waveform mix, a day later.
4. **Why the daily report became a public MCAST bulletin** (8 Oct). DL records the decision, PR #46 the design; neither gives the reason.
5. **Envelope frequency in v0.32.0.** The PR #235 title says "envelopes sent more often"; its body says "The envelope is back to one run per carrying slot, as on main". The body is later and more specific; the title is stale.
6. **Callsign change date.** Tom announced the move on 8 Oct (Discord 09:09) and said it would happen "following the next slot" on 11 Oct 11:24; DL and issue #83 put the switch at 11:16 on 11 Oct (first M0LTE slot 12:00). The journal does not print the callsign, so the date rests on DL and Discord. The Discord notes file calls the reason the FAC; the DL (6 Oct) says "the NoV may not cover 7.0524 to 7.0553". Both may be true; the public reason is the FAC wording.
7. **The 14 Oct retirement figures** (92 of 110 at 6 K, median K 5) come only from the decision log; I did not recount them from the journal.
8. **Who asked for the 8 Oct listener features** (#47-#54): the issues say "Raised from user feedback" without names.
