PILOT DIES IN FATAL CRASH INTO FLORIDA HAM TOWER

STEPHEN/ANCHOR: We begin our newscast this week with the fatal crash
of a helicopter into a ham radio tower in Florida. We hear more from
Kent Peterson KCÃDGY.

KENT: A veteran helicopter pilot was the only one aboard when, authorities
said, his helicopter struck a 150-foot-tall tower in back of a ham radio
operator's home in Myakka City, Florida. The 63-year-old pilot had just
completed a route of spraying for mosquito control shortly before 9 p.m.
when he was killed in the Oct. 15th accident in Manatee County,  A spokesman
for the county sheriff's office told local media that firefighters pulled
the pilot from the wreckage as it burned. He was pronounced dead at the
scene. The guyed tower - the tallest of three on the property - collapsed.


The Wireless Estimator, a website for the wireless infrastructure industry,
identified the property owner as Daniel Street, K1TO, who is also the
owner of an antenna installation company, A1 Tower Service. The website
noted that towers standing under 200 feet are not required by the Federal
Aviation Administration to be lit or to be registered with the FCC's
Antenna Structure Registration System. The Wireless Estimator said that
according to the public record, the towers were in compliance with those
rules.

The National Transportation Safety Board was investigating the incident
and typically issues a preliminary report within about 30 days after
a crash.

PRIVATELY DEVELOPED CUBESAT TRANSMITS AI EXPERIMENTS

STEPHEN/ANCHOR: The October 1st launch of a 2U CubeSat on a rideshare
mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California has distinguished
itself in a number of ways, as we hear from Jason Daniels VK2LAW.

JASON: Guan Hao Chan, KO6IKZ, is being celebrated by many in the CubeSat
community following the launch of KENSAT, a sun-synchronous satellite
he designed and built as a private individual, creating such support
systems as the ground station software. The radio amateur, who is from
Malaysia, has taken an uncommon route, not relying on any government
or big corporate funding. 

The CubeSat's payload includes what is being called an unusual artificial-intelligence
experiment in space: Using a neural network, KENSAT conducts machine
learning, downlinking the results via UHF to amateur ground stations.
According to the KENSAT website, the satellite is using a language model
known as Gemma 3 which [quote] "thinks in space and beams the answer
back to anyone listening." [endquote]

The IARU coordinated a downlink of 437.080 for the CubeSat, which is
using the same US callsign: KO6IKZ. The downlink uses GFSK modulation
with AX.25/G3RUH framing.

Chan said he hoped KENSAT would inspire other experimenters to attempt
independent CubeSat development. He also said he hoped hams would be
listening for the UHF transmissions as KENSAT makes its way in low-earth
orbit.

This is Jason Daniels VK2LAW.

(AMSAT NEWS, KENSAT.COM)

'PAYMENT LABYRINTH' CALLED OBSTACLE TO LICENSE RENEWALS

STEPHEN/ANCHOR: If you're needing to renew your FCC license soon - or
have tried but been unsuccessful in paying the $35 fee - you're not alone.
Andy Morrison K9AWM tells us about a study of FCC data that turned up
hundreds of failed amateur radio renewals - and lost callsigns.

ANDY:  Rob Rosenberger, K4HST, calls it the FCC's "payment labyrinth"
-- and he believes it is responsible for the failed attempts by 1 in
18 hams to complete their payment of the $35 license-renewal fee. He
came to his conclusions after analyzing the FCC's downloadable, publicly
accessible two-year archive starting in 2024.

In the US, the $35 fee applies to new license grants as well as renewals,
vanity call signs and any modifications, such as upgrades.

Rob, who is a retired systems analyst, said that his study shows that
some hams have actually had repeated failures to make their payments
- some as many as seven times. He said, however, [quote] "nine out of
ten folks who stumbled into the labyrinth decided not to map it out a
second time." [endquote] In the period he analyzed, he said, 3,901 hams
successfully renewed their callsign each month -- but another 783 simply
did not bother trying again and were lost to attrition..

Rob said the numbers point out the need for clubs and others, such as
the ARRL, to focus on the hams who fail the payment process in the ULS
system before they lose their callsign after the FCC's two-year grace
period. He said the solution begins at the local level - and ham radio
clubs can reduce the failure-to-pay rate through outreach to those who
may need assistance. His study has enabled him to produce free downloadable
PDFs that list all operators, sorted by city and state, who are within
three months of losing their callsign. The URL for the download is listed
in the text version of this week's newscast at arnewsline.org

for this important space explorer.

GRAHAM: The Australian Space Agency says "G'Day Moon."

Australia's first lunar rover -- that would be the Roo-ver -- met the
public recently during World Space Week at the Queensland University
of Technologyâs Lunar Testbed and Space Lab. Plans are to land the semi-autonomous

The trip will take place under NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services

Scan TV and Travis Lisk N3ILS is here to let us know who - or what -

TRAVIS: Slow Scan TV Series 34 aboard the International Space Station
