Now the Propagation news, compiled by G0KYA, G3YLA and G4BAO on 
Wednesday, the 7th of October 2026.

As we head further into October, the maximum usable frequencies 
available are slowly starting to climb. We are seeing 21MHz doing 
quite well, with 24MHz often coming into play. This should improve as 
the month goes on. 

This last week was characterised by relatively quiet geomagnetic 
conditions at times, but with a lowish Solar Flux Index. The Kp index 
hit four and five on the fourth and fifth but has otherwise been 
quiet, often in the ones and twos.

HF conditions have been adequate rather than outstanding. 80 metres 
has proved useful during the RSGB's Autumn Series of contests, often 
with good loud signals from around the UK and further afield from 
Austria, Germany and the Netherlands.

There have been some good highlights further up the bands, including 
hearing the 4S7B NCDXF beacon on 14.100MHz well after dark on Monday 
the 5th of October and the 4U1UN beacon on 21.150MHz during the late 
afternoon.

If you wish to work the eastern seaboard of the USA on 10 metres, 
look between 1300 and 1700UTC, with perhaps FT8 or CW being the most 
likely modes at this time.

There is plenty of DX to be worked next week, including 6O2M in 
Somalia, H49A on the Solomon Islands, C5R in The Gambia and FW1P, 
Wallis Island. From next weekend, also look for 3DAOGY in Eswatini, 
S21HRG in Bangladesh and S9R on Sao Tome.

The solar flux index climbed from 92 to 113 on Wednesday, the 7th of 
October, which is a help. The question is, where does it go from here?
Next week, the SWPC forecasts the SFI will be in the range of 90 to 
105. Geomagnetic conditions are forecast to be good, with a maximum 
Kp index of two. As always, this is subject to no CMEs or coronal 
holes, so keep an eye on solarham.com  for daily updates. This 
weekend may see unsettled geomagnetic conditions thanks to two large 
coronal holes.


And now the VHF and up propagation news from G3YLA and G4BAO.

We have moved into the season of autumn changeability with 
alternating unsettled weather. Rain scatter is possible on the GHz 
bands as is the traditional Tropo that comes from areas of high 
pressure and temperature inversions.  

The bulk of the period up to the end of next week is firmly in the 
unsettled domain with some potentially active lows passing close to 
the UK with perhaps some very strong winds in northern areas. So rain 
scatter is likely to be an option at times.

There is a signal in the weather models that we could see a big high 
developing at the end of next week, with Tropo returning to many 
areas.

The meteor scatter prospects are slightly lifted by the Draconids, 
which peaked on Thursday the 8th of October, and then the slow climb 
to the Orionids later in the month on Wednesday the 21st of October, 
which are the debris from Comet Halley and usually the better of the 
two showers.

We have recently been having the occasional peak in the Kp index to 
around 4 or 5, which is just enough to give us a reason to look out 
for aurora. Although not really where we need it for a good radio 
aurora, this is definitely a hint to check the Kp index.

The surprise out-of-season Sporadic-E on Monday, the 5th of October, 
when the FoEs reached just over 10MHz, was a reminder that guidelines 
about the span of the season are just that, and other externally 
forced events can and do happen. It is not easy to give exact reasons 
for that event, but a sudden increase in meteor input can play a 
regional role if other triggers like jet streams are also present. 
EME now, and Moon declination is negative and falling to minimum on 
Friday the 16th of October, so Moon windows are shortening and peak 
Moon elevation is falling. Path losses are rising as we approach 
apogee, also on the 16th. 

The Sun and Moon were close over the weekend of Saturday the 10th of 
October, so expect high sky noise, then moderate, becoming high again 
as the week progresses.

And that's all from the propagation team this week.
