Each year at the RSGB AGM, the Society gives three important trophies 
to groups or individuals who have made a significant national or 
international contribution to amateur radio. These trophies are: the 
Calcutta Key for work associated with international friendship 
through amateur radio; the Founders' Cup for outstanding service to 
the Society; and the Kenwood Trophy for making a significant 
contribution to training and development in amateur radio within the 
United Kingdom. This year, the RSGB Board would like to consider an 
even wider group of potential recipients when making its decision, so 
it is inviting you to suggest who should be considered for the RSGB 
2027 AGM trophies. To find out how to make your nomination, simply 
complete the form that you will find at the top of the RSGB trophies' 
web page at rsgb.org/trophies  The deadline for nominations is Monday 
the 4th of January 2027.

Now the Propagation News, compiled by G0KYA, G4BAO and G3YLA on 
Thursday the 22nd of October 2026

Autumnal HF conditions continue to improve with the odd surprise on 
the upper HF bands.

With a solar flux around 120, there have been some good HF contacts 
to be made via FT8 on 10 metres, including in Australia, China and 
Japan, which were all spotted during the afternoon. These openings 
have been fleeting but can easily add to your DXCC score.

Conditions have been variable, with a decreasing solar flux index and 
periods of geomagnetic disturbance, thanks to emissions from a 
coronal hole on the Sun. A Kp index of five or more usually means 
lower MUFs and noisier bands. 

But the time of year has helped with Autumnal HF conditions being 
some of the best. Maximum Usable Frequencies have usually been above 
21MHz during daylight hours, with some openings on 24 and 28MHz at 
times. If you venture up to 12 or 10 metres, give it some time as 
openings may be short and fleeting.

This week, DX to be worked includes YJ0CA in Vanuatu, TZ4AM in Mali, 
XX9TXN in Macao, 3DAOGY in Eswatini, S9R in Sao Tome, C5R in The 
Gambia and 5W0ZP in Samoa. Many other entities may remain on the air 
following the weekend's CQWW SSB Contest.

Also keep an eye on 40 and 80 metres, which should be improving as we 
enter late Autumn. Thirty metres, an overlooked band, may be 
currently open all night, so it is worth a look.

Next week, NOAA predicts that the SFI will remain around the 95 to 
105 mark. Geomagnetic conditions are predicted to be good all week 
with a Kp index of two. This may increase to five at the end of the 
month, so do your DXing early in the week. As always, keep an eye on 
solarham.com as it only takes one solar flare and associated CME to 
push the Kp index up.


And now the VHF and up propagation news from G3YLA and G4BAO.

The middle of last week saw some good daytime rainscatter on 24GHz 
for those of us fortunate enough to catch it. More active weather 
fronts are likely to bring spells of heavy rain or showers, which 
might cause a repeat, so keep an eye on the rain maps if you have 
equipment for the 5.7, 10 and 24GHz bands.

The current spell of unsettled weather is likely to run through much 
of the coming week and, in typical autumn fashion, some of these 
storms will be major lows with strong or potentially damaging winds 
for unsecured aerials.  

The recent slight enhancements of the Kp index suggest that we should 
still keep an eye out for some probably weak auroral activity if the 
Kp index goes above five.  

On to meteor scatter now and it seems that we have recently seen a 
top-up of meteor input due to the peak of the Orionid meteor shower, 
which peaked last Wednesday night. That may have helped the 50MHz 
Sporadic Es QSOs on the 17th of October for some. QSOs were made on 
FT8 into Iberia, Scandinavia and North Africa where 7T5E was active. 
Cloudy weather probably made it difficult to see evidence of the 
meteors but meteor scatter on the VHF bands may well reveal the extra 
activity and hopefully leave some residual meteors for the coming 
week. 

EME now, and Moon declination is now positive and rising to a maximum 
on Thursday the 29th of October. Moon windows lengthen and peak Moon 
elevation is rising. Perigee is on Wednesday the 28th when path 
losses are at their lowest. 144 MHz sky noise is low, rising to 
moderate on the 30th.

And that's all from the propagation team this week.
