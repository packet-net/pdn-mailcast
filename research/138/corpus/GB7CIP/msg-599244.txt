Onshore flow and upper-level troughing will produce light rain
over parts of the Pacific Northwest with higher-elevation snow and
lower-elevation rain over the Cascades, the Northern Intermountain
Region, and the Northern Rockies through Thursday. Meanwhile, a
front moving westward across Florida and onshore flow will produce
showers and thunderstorms over southern Florida and along the
eastern coast through Thursday.

Moreover, easterly flow off the Gulf will aid in producing showers
and thunderstorms over the Western Gulf Coast through Wednesday
evening. Upper-level energy will create heavy rain over southern
Texas overnight Wednesday into Thursday. Therefore, the WPC has
issued a Slight Risk (level 2/4) of excessive rainfall over
southern Texas with these thunderstorms. The associated heavy rain
will create mainly localized areas of flash flooding, with urban
areas, roads, small streams, narrow canyons/gullies, and burn
scars the most vulnerable.

A wave of low pressure over the Northern Plains/Upper Mississippi
Valley will slowly dissipate by Tuesday evening, while producing
rain over parts of the Upper Mississippi Valley and
Central/Southern Plains on Tuesday morning into early afternoon. A
second wave of low pressure over the Northern High Plains will
produce rain over parts of the Northern/Central Plains and the
Upper/Middle Mississippi Valley on Wednesday morning. Showers and
thunderstorms will develop over the Upper Mississippi Valley and
Upper Great Lakes on Wednesday evening. By Thursday morning, rain
will develop along and ahead of the boundary over parts of the
Great Lakes and into the Middle Mississippi Valley.
