WATCHES AND WARNINGS
--------------------
There are no coastal watches and warnings in effect.

Interests in southwestern and west-central Mexico, as well as Baja
California Sur should continue to monitor the progress of Trudy.


DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 800 PM MST (0300 UTC), the center of Hurricane Trudy was located
near latitude 19.7 North, longitude 107.6 West. Trudy is moving
toward the northwest near 7 mph (11 km/h). A turn toward the
west-northwest or west is expected tonight into Monday. On the
forecast track, the center of Trudy will move further away from the
coast of mainland Mexico during the overnight hours, then move south
of Baja California Sur on Monday and Tuesday.

Maximum sustained winds have decreased to near 80 mph (130 km/h)
with higher gusts. Rapid weakening is expected to continue tonight
into Monday, and Trudy could degenerate to a post-tropical low by
Tuesday night.

Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 25 miles (35 km) from the
center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 90 miles
(150 km).

The estimated minimum central pressure is 984 mb (29.06 inches).


HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Trudy can be found in the Tropical Cyclone

and on the web at hurricanes.gov/graphics_ep1.shtml?key_messages.

RAINFALL: Through tonight, Trudy is expected to produce up to an
additional 1 to 2 inches of rain along and near the southwest
Mexican coast in Jalisco.

For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with Trudy,
please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall Graphic
available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_ep1.shtml?rainqpf.

SURF: Swells generated by Trudy are affecting portions of the coast
of southwestern and west-central Mexico during the next two to
three days. These swells will cause life-threatening surf and rip
current conditions. Please consult products from your local weather
office.
