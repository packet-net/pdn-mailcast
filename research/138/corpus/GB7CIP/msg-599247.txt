Onshore flow and upper-level troughing will produce light rain
over parts of the Pacific Northwest with higher-elevation snow and
lower-elevation rain over the Cascades, the Northern Intermountain
Region, and the Northern Rockies through Thursday morning. On
Friday morning, a front will move onshore over the Northwest,
bringing rain and high-elevation snow over parts of the Pacific
Northwest and the Northern Intermountain Region. Meanwhile, a
front moving westward across Florida, along with onshore flow,
will produce showers and thunderstorms over parts of Florida
through Friday.

Moreover, flow off the Gulf will aid in producing showers and
thunderstorms over the Western Gulf Coast through Wednesday
afternoon. In addition, upper-level energy will move over the
region, creating heavy rain over the Southern High Plains
overnight Wednesday into Friday. Therefore, the WPC has issued a
Slight Risk (level 2/4) of excessive rainfall over the Southern
High Plains with these thunderstorms on Thursday through Friday
morning. The associated heavy rain will create mainly localized
areas of flash flooding, with urban areas, roads, small streams,
narrow canyons/gullies, and burn scars the most vulnerable.

Meanwhile, a front over the Upper Midwest will produce showers and
thunderstorms over parts of the Northern Plains and Upper
Mississippi Valley on Wednesday afternoon and move eastward into
the Upper Great Lakes by Wednesday evening.  The showers and
thunderstorms move into the Middle Mississippi Valley and Central
Plains by Thursday morning. More rain will develop along the front
over parts of the Great Lakes into the Middle Mississippi Valley
on Thursday morning and over the Lower Great Lakes by Thursday
evening and into parts of the Northeast by Friday morning.

Furthermore, a surge of moisture off the Gulf of California will
produce showers and thunderstorms with heavy rain over the
Southwest on Friday. Therefore, the WPC has issued a Slight Risk
(level 2/4) of excessive rainfall over the Southwest with these
thunderstorms. The associated heavy rain will create mainly
localized areas of flash flooding, with urban areas, roads, small
streams, narrow canyons/gullies, and burn scars the most
vulnerable. Additionally, some showers and thunderstorms will also
develop over parts of southeastern California on Friday.
