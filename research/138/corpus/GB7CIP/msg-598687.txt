On October 23, 2001, Apple introduces the iPod, a portable digital music player that revolutionized the music industry and transformed the consumption of audio and eventually video content. Its minimalist, user-friendly design also helped reshape Apple from primarily a computer company to a consumer device titan and paved the way for the iPhone.

As digital music gained popularity, consumers traded portable cassette and CD players for MP3 players, but the early models were not user-friendly and could hold only a limited number of songs. Seeing a market opportunity, Apple CEO Steve Jobs brought Tony Fadell on board to help develop the iPod in early 2001. The team worked on an aggressive timeline to get the device on store shelves in time for the holiday season. They beat their deadline, and the original iPod went on sale on November 10, 2001.

About the size of a deck of cards, the first iPod featured a center selection button and four additional control buttons circling its signature mechanical scroll wheel, which let users browse by turning the wheel. Its clean interface, which even lacked an on/off switch, along with its white front, stainless steel back and trademark white earbuds helped make the iPod a design icon.
