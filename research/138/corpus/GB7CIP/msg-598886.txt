Tropical Weather Discussion
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1215 UTC Sun Oct 25 2026

Tropical Weather Discussion for North America, Central America
Gulf of America, Caribbean Sea, northern sections of South
America, and Atlantic Ocean to the African coast from the
Equator to 31N. The following information is based on satellite
imagery, weather observations, radar and meteorological analysis.

Based on <a href='https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/tafb/USA_06Z.gif'>0600 UT
1030 UTC.

...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Atlantic Gale Warning: A tightening pressure gradient will
continue to develop across the western and central offshore zones
today through this evening as strong high pressure builds
southward over the western Atlantic, and low pressure deepens ENE
of the central Bahamas, and just S of a stationary front along
27N-30N. This setup is expected to induce widespread strong to
near gale-force NE to E winds and very rough seas building to 20
ft, with gale-force winds expected over much of the waters north
of 27N and between 60W and 80W today through early Mon morning.
The front is expected to stall from near Bermuda to the central
Bahamas on Mon, with winds and seas diminishing very slowly
through Thu. Mariners are urged to remain alert to these dangerous
marine conditions, and take the necessary precautions to avoid
these affected areas.

Please, see the latest NWS High Seas
Forecast issued by the National Hurricane Center at website
https:<a href='https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFAT2.shtml'>www.nh

Heavy Rainfall in the Northern Caribbean: A mid to upper level
trough over Florida, the NW Caribbean and the Bahamas combined
with abundant tropical moisture will support periods of heavy rain
over eastern Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica, the southern Bahamas, and
the Turks and Caicos Islands through Mon. Today, model guidance
indicates that the trough will begin to weaken and meander
northward. The main areas that are expected to be impacted by
abundant tropical moisture and heavy rainfall will then shift to
north and east of Cuba over the southern Bahamas, Turks and
Caicos, the Windward Passage and Hispaniola. The heavy rainfall
will raise concerns for dangerous and life-threatening flash
flooding. Please refer to your local weather services and the
International Desk at the Weather Prediction Center for more
information.

...MONSOON TROUGH/ITCZ...

The monsoon trough axis extends from the coast of Africa near
14N16W southwestward to 07N27W. The ITCZ extends from 10N43W to
10N60W. Scattered moderate to isolated strong convection is seen
from 04N to 25N between 20W-41W, and from 09N to 14N between
56W-61W.

...GULF OF AMERICA...

A strong ridge extends over most of the eastern United States and
into the central Gulf, supporting moderate to locally fresh NE to
E winds across most of the E Gulf region, except locally strong E
of 86W. Moderate to fresh ENE winds are elsewhere. Moderate seas
prevail, with locally rough seas offshore NW Florida.

For the forecast, a tightening pressure gradient between the high
pressure and a low pressure forecast to deepen near the central
Bahamas will bring fresh to strong NE winds and rough seas over
the eastern Gulf, including the Florida Straits through early Wed.

...CARIBBEAN SEA...

Please see the Special Features section above for information
about the potential for heavy rainfall over eastern Cuba,
Hispaniola, and Jamaica, the Turks and Caicos and locations farther
east through the early part of the upcoming week.

A broad surface trough over the central Bahamas extends southward
across E Cuba to a 1009 mb low E of Jamaica near 19N75W. Numerous
moderate to strong convection is ahead of this low under an area
of upper level diffluence that covers Hispaniola and adjacent
waters. Gust to gale force winds and rough seas are likely within
this area of convection south of Hispaniola between 68W and 75W.
Winds over the NW and portions of SW Caribbean are moderate to
fresh from the NNW while ENE winds of the same speeds are E of
72W. Seas over these regions are moderate and slight between 74W
and 78W where winds are light to gentle and variable due to the
low pressure E of Jamaica.

For the forecast, the mid to upper level trough over Florida, the
NW Caribbean and the Bahamas will shift very slowly eastward
through Mon and support scattered to numerous showers and
thunderstorms over the central Caribbean and Windward Passage.
The broad surface trough over the central Bahamas that extends
across E Cuba to a 1009 mb low E of Jamaica will drift eastward
through Tue. Strong high pressure across the eastern U.S. will
continue to build southward across Florida and the Gulf of America
through early next week, leading to moderate to fresh N winds and
moderate seas across the western Caribbean W of Jamaica through
late Tue. Expect rough seas in large N swell across the Yucatan
Channel late tonight through early Tue. Otherwise, moderate to
fresh ESE winds in the E Caribbean will diminish to gentle to
moderate speeds by Mon evening.

...ATLANTIC OCEAN...

Please see the Special Features section above for information
about a Gale Warning over the western Atlantic, and on heavy
rainfall expected for the southern Bahamas, the Turks and Caicos
Islands, the Windward Passage, and Hispaniola.

A stationary front extends from near 31N48W to 28N65W. There is a
1013 mb low just E of the central Bahamas while earlier scatt data
also showed the presence of a stronger low of 1012 mb south of the
front near 26N65W. A surface trough then extends southward from
this low to Hispaniola adjacent waters. These features are
supporting scattered moderate convection across the Atlantic
offshores, except S of 24N between 55W and 63W. Widespread strong
to gale-force winds and rough seas are north of the front or
across many of the offshores N of 27N. Otherwise, the Azores
ridge dominates the remainder Atlantic subtropical waters,
providing mainly moderate or weaker winds and moderate seas,
except for strong winds funneling through the Canary Islands where
rough seas are ongoing.

For the forecast west of 55W, a tightening pressure gradient will
continue to develop across the western and central offshore zones
today through this evening as strong high pressure builds
southward over the western Atlantic, and low pressure deepens ENE
of the central Bahamas, and just S of a stationary front along
27N-30N. This setup is expected to induce widespread strong to
near gale-force NE to E winds and very rough seas building to 20
ft, with gale-force winds expected over much of the waters north
of 27N and between 60W and 80W today through early Mon morning.
The front is expected to stall from near Bermuda to the central
Bahamas on Mon, with winds and seas diminishing very slowly
through Thu.
