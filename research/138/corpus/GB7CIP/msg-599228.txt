Solar Activity

.24 hr Summary...
Solar activity continued at low levels with the largest flare being a
C1.3 at 23/1907 UTC from the only active region with sunspots on disk:
Region 4560 (N13W65, Eao/beta-gamma). Decay continued to be observed in
its intermediate spots over the past 24 hours.

No new Earth-directed CMEs were observed in available coronagraph
imagery.

.Forecast...
Solar activity is expected to remain at low levels through 26 Oct, with
a slight chance for isolated M-class flares (R1-R2, Minor-Moderate).

Energetic Particle

.24 hr Summary...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux reached high levels in the past
24h, with max flux of 3,169 pfu at 23/1540 UTC. The greater than 10 MeV
proton flux remained steady at background levels.

.Forecast...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux is expected to continue reaching
high levels through 26 Oct. The greater than 10 MeV proton flux is
anticipated to persist at background levels through 26 Oct.

Solar Wind

.24 hr Summary...
Solar wind parameters reflected diminishing positive polarity CH HSS
influences. Total magnetic field strength (Bt) was about 5 nT with the
Bz component mostly negative, briefly observed as far south as -6 nT
around 23/1740 UTC. Solar wind speeds maintained between ~400-450 km/s.
Phi angle was predominantly in the positive solar sector (away from the
Sun).

.Forecast...
Solar wind conditions are expected to continue at similar levels over 24
Oct. Enhanced solar wind conditions are likely on 25-26 Oct due to
combined influences of a negative polarity CH HSS and possible glancing
CME effects from the 21 Oct eruptions.

Geospace

.24 hr Summary...
Geomagnetic activity was mostly quiet during the period, reaching active
levels during an isolated synoptic period (23/1800-2100 UTC) due to a
sustained Bz negative period.

.Forecast...
Quiet to unsettled conditions are expected on 24 Oct as CH HSS
influences wane. Periods of active conditions, with a chance for G1
(Minor) storming, are anticipated on 25-26 Oct due to combined effects
from a negative polarity coronal hole and potential glancing CME
impacts.
