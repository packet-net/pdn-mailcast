Tropical Weather Discussion for North America, Central America
Gulf of America, Caribbean Sea, northern sections of South
America, and Atlantic Ocean to the African coast from the
Equator to 31N. The following information is based on satellite
imagery, weather observations, radar and meteorological analysis.

Based on <a href='https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/tafb/USA_12Z.gif'>1200 UT
1730 UTC.

...MONSOON TROUGH/ITCZ...

The monsoon trough axis enters the Atlantic through the coast of
The Gambia near 13N17W, and continues west-southwestward to
08N31W, where it transitions to the ITCZ to 08N40W and to
near 09N51W. Scattered moderate convection is within 60 nm
of the trough between the coast of Africa and 17W. Similar
activity is within 60 nm south of the trough between 24W-27W.

...GULF OF AMERICA...

A trough is in the SW Gulf from near 21N95W to 18N94W. Scattered
showers and isolated thunderstorms are within 60 nm west of the
trough from 19N to 21N, and within 90 nm east of the trough south
of 19N. Another trough extends from near Corpus Christi to 27N96W
and to 24N96.5W. Isolated showers and thunderstorms are within 30
nm east of the trough from 27N to 28N. Fresh to strong northwest
winds are west of the SW Gulf trough as high pressure southward
over Mexico. Seas with these winds are about 8 to 9 ft. Broad high
pressure is elsewhere over the basin, with the resultant pressure
gradient generally allowing for moderate to fresh northeast to
east winds east of about 94W, and gentle to moderate northeast
winds elsewhere west of 94W. Seas are mostly in the moderate
range.

For the forecast, the fresh to strong winds west of the SW Gulf
will diminish to fresh speeds late tonight. A tightening pressure
gradient between the ridge and a low pressure forecast to develop
near the Bahamas will bring fresh to strong northeast winds and
moderate to rough seas over the eastern Gulf Fri night into Sat.
These marine conditions will spread westward to the central Gulf
Sat night through Sun night. Seas are expected to build to around
13 ft with these winds over the southeastern Gulf.

...CARIBBEAN SEA...

Scattered to numerous showers and thunderstorms are persistent
offshore Colombia to near 13N between 73W and 77W. High pressure
north of the area and a nearby stationary frontal boundary support
moderate to fresh NE winds and seas of 2-4 ft in the waters north
of 20N. Moderate to fresh trades along with seas of 3 to 5 ft are
found from 11.5N to 16N and east of 80W. Elsewhere, light to
gentle winds and seas of 2 to 4 ft prevail.

For the forecast, a weak pressure gradient across the basin will
promote gentle to moderate winds through Fri. The pressure
gradient between a developing low pressure near the Bahamas and
Atlantic high pressure will induce moderate to fresh east to
southeast winds over the eastern Caribbean beginning Fri night.
This weather pattern will also produce gentle to moderate
northerly winds over the western part of the basin, with the
exception of moderate to fresh northeast winds in the Yucatan
Channel, and mainly light to gentle winds over the central
Caribbean. These marine conditions are forecast to last through
the upcoming weekend.

...ATLANTIC OCEAN...

A stationary front is analyzed from 31N64W to 27N70W and to the
central Bahamas and to central Cuba. Scattered showers are
occurring near this boundary. Ahead of it, a trough extends from
near 26N68W to 23N72W and to the Windward Passage. Numerous
moderate convection is seen from 22N to 27N between 66W and 72W.
Overnight scatterometer data highlighted moderate to fresh
north to northeast winds behind the front. Seas over these waters
are in the 8 to 13 ft range per latest satellite altimeter data
and buoy observations. Farther east, weak low pressure of 1012 mb
is near 28N57W. It is along a NW to SE oriented trough that
extends from near 3163W to the low and continues southeastward to
near 24N54W. The combination of these features with a broad upper-
level trough in the vicinity will continue to result in a large
area of numerous to isolated strong convection over the waters
from 22N to 30N between 45W and 57W. Moderate to fresh southerly
winds along with moderate seas are east of the surface trough.

Meanwhile, well east of the Lesser Antilles, an area of scattered
showers and a few thunderstorms is from 12N to 17N between 47W
and 54W. Isolated showers are from 13N to 18N between 38W and 47W.
To the NE, a trough extends from 30N24W to 22N23W. No deep
convection is noted with this feature. Latest scatterometer data
shows light to gentle northeast to east winds within 180 nm either
side of the trough from 22N to 24N and moderate to fresh northeast
to east winds north of 24N between 16W and 34W. Seas over these
waters are 5 to 7 ft. The remainder of the tropical Atlantic east
of about 55W is under the influence of strong high pressure
ridging, with the parent high center of 1031 mb located to the
north of the Azores. The related pressure gradient is maintaining
moderate or weaker winds along with moderate seas.

For the forecast west of 55W, moderate to fresh N to NE winds and
NE swell persist west of a stationary front that extends from
near Bermuda to the central Bahamas and to central Cuba. Winds
will diminish to gentle to moderate speeds later today while seas
will slowly subside across the waters W of 65W and NE of the
Bahamas through Fri. A tightening pressure gradient will develop
beginning late on Fri as high pressure builds southward over the
western Atlantic and low pressure develops near the Bahamas along
a remnant frontal trough. This setup is expected to induce
widespread fresh to strong northeast to east winds and rough to
very rough seas, with winds likely reaching gale-force over the
waters roughly NW of a line from near 31N60W to the northern
Bahamas on Sun. Mariners should monitor the latest marine
forecasts, and have avoidance plans in place.
