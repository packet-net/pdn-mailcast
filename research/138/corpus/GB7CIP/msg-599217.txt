NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
353 AM EDT Thu Oct 22 2026

Valid 12Z Thu Oct 22 2026 - 12Z Sat Oct 24 2026

...Conditions improve along the East Coast today as Subtropical
Storm Josephine moves farther out to sea...

...Excessive rainfall and severe weather impacts California, the
Southwest, and Four Corners...


Subtropical Storm Josephine begins to track farther away from the
East Coast and into the Western Atlantic today. NHC anticipates
that Josephine will likely transition to a Post-Tropical cyclone
later today. A brief period of storm force wind gusts (50  kts)
are possible near the ocean waters southeast of Nantucket. Inland
areas of southern New England could see gusts closer to 20-45 mph.
Beach erosion remains possible due to strong onshore winds and
large waves. Conditions are expected to improve across the broader
Mid-Atlantic/Northeast Coast today.

A deep upper-level trough will enter the Western U.S. today. This
system will pull very anomalous tropical moisture and spread it
out over Central/Southern California, the Great Basin and Four
Corners regions today. A cold front at the surface will move
through the interior West and act as a focus for showers and
thunderstorms throughout the day. Heavy rainfall is expected
across the Four Corners with the heaviest precipitation occurring
over southern facing terrain. A moderate risk (at least 40chance) of excessive rainfall is in effect for portions of
southern Utah into southwestern Colorado. A broader slight risk
(at least 15hance) is in effect across the Four Corners states
and into southern Nevada. Dry washes, slot canyons and burn scars
are especially susceptible to flash flooding. The excessive
rainfall threat shifts into the Southern/Central High Plains on
Friday and Saturday.

Some instability may work its way into the Four Corners and
Southwest, which could support severe thunderstorms, although the
greater threat for severe storms will likely be over southeastern
Arizona and into southwestern New Mexico, where a slight risk
(level 2/5) of severe weather was issued by the Storm Prediction
Center. Hail and severe wind gusts are possible. Heavy Snow is
also possible for parts of the southern Sierra Nevada Mountains
and Intermountain West/Colorado Rockies today. This threat shifts
into the Northern Rockies on Friday behind a new surface low
pressure system that intensifies while diving south through the
Northwest.

Temperatures will be below average in the West thanks to the
unsettled weather. An amplifying upper-level ridge will promote
above average to well above average temps with some record
minimums possible over parts of the Northern/Central Plains and
Upper Midwest. Surface high pressure over the East will support
clear skies as well as average to below average temperatures
through this weekend. Freeze and frost are possible for many areas
given the clear skies overnight. Shortwave energy will dive into
the Florida Peninsula tonight and generate showers and
thunderstorms for southern portions of the state over the coming
days, with some potential for excessive rainfall emerging by the
weekend.
