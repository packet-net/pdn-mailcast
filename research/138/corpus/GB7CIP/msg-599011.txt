Tropical Weather Discussion for North America, Central America
Gulf of America, Caribbean Sea, northern sections of South
America, and Atlantic Ocean to the African coast from the
Equator to 31N. The following information is based on satellite
imagery, weather observations, radar and meteorological analysis.

Based on <a href='https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/tafb/USA_12Z.gif'>1200 UT
1630 UTC.

...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Atlantic Gale Warning: A tight pressure gradient between a 1031 mb h
pressure system over the eastern United States and a stationary
front extending from near Bermuda to the NW Bahamas supports a
large area of strong winds, with frequent gusts to gale force,
across much of the waters N of 27N and W of the front. Rough to
very rough seas in N to NE swell follow the front. The portion of
the front east of 70W is expected to begin to drift southeastward
Tue through Wed night as a cold front before becoming stationary
again late this week. The aforementioned ridge will shift slowly
eastward into the north Atlantic during this time. The frequent
gusts to gale-force will diminish tonight, however, fresh to strong
winds and rough to very rough seas in NE swell to the north of
the front will persist through the period. Mariners are urged to
remain alert to these dangerous marine conditions and take the
necessary precautions to avoid these affected areas. Please, see
the latest NWS High Seas Forecast issued by the National
Hurricane Center at website
https:<a href='https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFAT2.shtml'>www.nh

Heavy Rainfall in the Northern Caribbean: Deep-layer troughing
extending southward through the Bahamas into the NW Caribbean
combined with abundant tropical moisture will support periods of
heavy rainfall over eastern Cuba, Hispaniola, the southern
Bahamas, and the Turks and Caicos Islands into midweek. The trough
will gradually shift N and E early this week, while weakening.
Very heavy rainfall will bring a potential for dangerous and life-
threatening flash flooding, particularly over portion of eastern
Cuba, Haiti, and the Dominican Republic. Please refer to your
local weather services and the International Desk at the Weather
Prediction Center for more information.

...MONSOON TROUGH/ITCZ...

The monsoon trough enters the Atlantic near 14.5N17W and extends
southwestward to 07.5N25W. The ITCZ extends from 07.5N25W to
07N41W, and then from 09N50W to 10N60W. A surface trough is
analyzed from 14N44W to 06N45W. Scattered moderate convection is
observed from 04N to 10N and between 22W and 30W.

...GULF OF AMERICA...

A tight pressure gradient between a 1031 mb high pressure system
over the eastern United States and a stationary front extending
into the Bahamas supports fresh to strong NE-E winds and rough
seas over the E Gulf waters, including the Florida Straits.
Moderate or weaker winds and moderate seas prevail elsewhere.

For the forecast, a tight pressure gradient between a 1031 mb
high pressure system over the eastern United States and a
stationary front extending from near Bermuda to the NW Bahamas
supports fresh to strong NE to E winds and rough seas over the E
Gulf waters, including the Florida Straits. This weather pattern
will persist into midweek, followed by diminishing winds and seas
into next weekend. Elsewhere, gentle to moderate winds and
moderate seas will prevail during much of the workweek.

...CARIBBEAN SEA...

See Special Features section above for information on heavy
rainfall over eastern Cuba and Hispaniola.

An upper-level low spinning over central Cuba combined with
abundant tropical moisture supports a band of shower with embedded
thunderstorms across the central Caribbean and Hispaniola,
including also the Windward Passage. Moderate to locally fresh
easterly winds and moderate seas are occurring east of 72W.
Moderate to locally fresh NW to N winds are also occurring over
the Gulf of Honduras along with seas 3 to 5 ft. Elsewhere,
moderate or weaker winds and slight to moderate seas prevail.

For the forecast, the upper-level low will drift eastward through
mid- week keeping the chance of showers and thunderstorms over
the central Caribbean and Hispaniola. Meanwhile, the surface
trough over the central Caribbean will drift westward over the
next few days. Strong high pressure across the eastern U.S. will
continue to build southward across Florida and the Gulf of America
through midweek, leading to moderate to fresh N winds and
moderate seas across the western Caribbean W of the trough through
late Tue. Expect rough seas in large north swell across the
Yucatan Channel through early Tue. Otherwise, moderate to fresh E
to SE winds in the E Caribbean will diminish to gentle to moderate
speeds by tonight.

...ATLANTIC OCEAN...

Please see the Special Features section above for information
about a Gale Warning over the western Atlantic, and on heavy
rainfall expected for the Turks and Caicos Islands and
Hispaniola.

Scattered to numerous showers and thunderstorms are occurring
between 64W and 78.5W in association with the stationary front
and surface troughs described in the Special Features section.
South of the front, moderate to fresh SE to S winds and rough to
very rough seas are occurring and extend eastward to 60W. Farther
east, a cold front stretches across the central Atlantic and
moderate easterly winds and rough seas are noted north of 29N and
betwen 38W and 53W. The remainder of the basin is under a broad
subtropical ridge that supports moderate to locally fresh easterly
trades winds south of 25N and seas of 5-8 ft. Elsewhere, light to
gentle winds and moderate seas are prevalent.

For the forecast west of 55W, a tight pressure gradient between a
1031 mb high pressure system over the eastern United States and a
stationary front extending from near Bermuda to the NW Bahamas
supports a large area of strong winds, with frequent gusts to gale
force, across much of the waters N of 27N and W of the front.
Rough to very rough seas in N to NE swell follow the front. The
portion of the front east of 70W is expected to begin to drift
southeastward Tue through Wed night as a cold front before
becoming stationary again late this week. The aforementioned ridge
will shift slowly eastward into the north Atlantic during this
time. The frequent gusts to gale-force will diminish tonight,
however, fresh to strong winds and rough to very rough seas in NE
swell to the north of the front will persist through the period.
Mariners are urged to remain alert to these dangerous marine
conditions and take the necessary precautions to avoid these
affected areas.
