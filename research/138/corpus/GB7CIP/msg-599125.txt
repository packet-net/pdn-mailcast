WATCHES AND WARNINGS
--------------------
There are no coastal watches or warnings in effect.

Interests along the southwestern and west-central coasts of Mexico
and the southern Baja California Peninsula should monitor the
progress of Vance.


DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 800 AM MST (1500 UTC), the center of Tropical Storm Vance was
located near latitude 16.3 North, longitude 107.7 West. Vance is
moving toward the northwest near 8 mph (13 km/h). A northwestward
motion is expected to continue today, followed by a gradual turn
toward the north through midweek.

Maximum sustained winds have decreased to near 40 mph (65 km/h) with
higher gusts. Little change in strength is forecast during the next
couple of days, and Vance could become a post-tropical cyclone
during this time if it is unable to sustain organized convection.

Tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 175 miles (280 km)
primarily to the southeast of the center.

The estimated minimum central pressure is 1003 mb (29.62 inches).


HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
RAINFALL: Through Thursday, Vance is expected to produce 2 to 4
inches of rain across central and southern Sinaloa, western Durango,
and northwestern Nayarit, with isolated higher totals of 7 inches
possible over central and southern Sinaloa. This rainfall may
produce flooding and mudslides, especially in areas along steep
terrain.

For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with Vance,
please see the National Weather Service Storm Total Rainfall Graphic
available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_ep2.shtml?rainqpf.

SURF: Swells generated by Vance will affect coastal portions of
southern, southwestern, and west-central Mexico during the next few
days. These swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip
current conditions. Please consult products from your local weather
office.
